# Search & Destroy Companion review standard

Apply this complete ten-role board to meaningful product changes. The six later roles add to the original four; they never replace them.

1. CODM and S&D specialist: verify mechanics, terminology, tradeoffs, and situational advice.
2. Technical developer: protect maintainability, routes, versioning, caching, offline behavior, and deployment integrity.
3. Product and UX critic: solve a real player problem, keep the Starter focused, and make the next action obvious.
4. Editor and teacher: use ordinary language first, preserve legitimate game terms, define them, and teach without condescension.
5. Accessibility specialist: check cognitive load, contrast, text, touch, focus, keyboard, reduced motion, screen-reader meaning, and narrow mobile geometry.
6. Competitive analyst: ensure beginner guidance remains sound at higher skill levels and label advanced exceptions.
7. Quality-assurance tester: test routes, content references, persistence, offline/update behavior, runtime errors, and rendered interaction flows.
8. Researcher and fact-checker: verify current claims with primary evidence or repeatable tests and label uncertainty honestly.
9. Marketing and growth strategist: describe the audience and value truthfully, distinguish distribution signals, and synthesize feedback without overreacting.
10. Security and privacy reviewer: minimize data, protect private information, and review links, scripts, dependencies, storage, permissions, and updates.

## Product guardrails

- Teach COD Mobile Search & Destroy without requiring hostile trial and error.
- Keep the Starter focused; retain the Full Companion for deeper help.
- Do not reintroduce inaccurate map visuals or team-coach framing without an explicit product decision.
- Use ordinary language first while retaining established CODM and S&D terminology with definitions or dictionary support.
- Inspect the repository and deployment before acting. Conversation history describes intent, not current state.
- Distinguish Built, Verified, Committed, Published, and Live.
- Do not publish, delete, or make risky external changes without action-time authorization.

## Release gate

Before recommending a release, check all ten roles. Run relevant automated tests and rendered narrow-mobile checks. Verify version/build/cache synchronization and service-worker coverage. Report exactly what passed, failed, remained uncertain, or could not be tested.
